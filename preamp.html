Monophonic Phonograph Preamplifier

A high‑fidelity phonograph preamplifier designed for both Moving Magnet (MM) and Moving Coil (MC) cartridges. This project integrates RIAA equalisation, low‑noise op‑amp stages, relay‑based mode switching, and a compact, manufacturable PCB layout.

Design Overview

The preamplifier was designed to meet the RIAA equalisation standard using an inverting amplifier topology. This allows the RC networks to sit directly in the feedback loop, enabling precise control of the required time constants:

τZ = 318 μs, τP1 = 75 μs, τP2 = 3180 μs

Using LTSpice, resistor and capacitor values were tuned to achieve <1% deviation from the ideal RIAA curve. The MM stage provides 36 dB gain, while the MC stage adds an additional 20 dB using a non‑inverting op‑amp configuration.

RIAA Equalisation

The RIAA network was implemented using two RC pairs in the feedback loop of an OPA1641 op‑amp. This JFET‑input amplifier was selected for its low noise, high slew rate, and excellent linearity.

Component values:

  • R1 = 73.2 kΩ
  • C1 = 43 nF
  • R2 = 6.19 kΩ
  • C2 = 12 nF
  • Rin = 1.27 kΩ

These values produce a closed‑loop gain of 35.9 dB, matching the MM requirement.

MM / MC Mode Switching

A DPDT relay enables switching between MM and MC modes:

  • One pole switches the gain stage between unity (MM) and gain of 10 (MC)
  • The other pole switches input loading between MM (47.5 kΩ, 100 pF) and MC (100 Ω, 3000 pF)

This ensures correct cartridge loading and accurate reproduction of the RIAA curve for both cartridge types.

PCB Layout

A compact two‑layer PCB (55.9 mm × 33.6 mm) was designed with:

  • 10 mil minimum track width and spacing
  • 39 mil power tracks for reduced resistance and noise
  • SMD components for density and low parasitics
  • Through‑hole connectors for durability
  • Careful placement to minimise noise coupling from the relay coil

Mounting holes (2.2 mm) were added for secure enclosure integration.

Performance

LTSpice simulations were used to evaluate distortion, noise, and power consumption.

Total Harmonic Distortion

THD was measured at 1.157% for both MM and MC modes. Reducing resistor values (e.g., R5) may further improve linearity.

Noise

Noise spectral density:

  • MM: 1.4 μV/√Hz
  • MC: 9.6 μV/√Hz

R5 dominates noise due to its position at the inverting input.

Power Consumption

MM mode: 86.4 mW total MC mode: 98 mW total

The push‑pull output stage explains the slight rail asymmetry at higher gain.
