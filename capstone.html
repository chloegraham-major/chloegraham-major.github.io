Temperature–Irradiance–Spectrum Modelling for Emerging PVs

This project develops a quantitative modelling framework that explains how emerging photovoltaic technologies behave under real‑world operating conditions. Drawing on my interest in energy systems, modelling and market‑relevant analysis, I adapted the De Soto five‑parameter model—traditionally used for silicon PVs—to accurately represent perovskite and organic devices across temperature, irradiance and spectral variation.

The work reflects my broader engineering approach: building analytical tools that help us understand not just what happens in an energy system, but why it happens, and how those technical behaviours influence commercial and system‑level outcomes.

Project Motivation

Standard AM1.5 testing provides a narrow view of PV performance and fails to capture the conditions that matter most in real installations — high temperatures, low irradiance, cloud cover and spectral shifts. Recent research shows that emerging PV technologies can outperform expectations in these environments, narrowing the gap with silicon and, in some cases, achieving near‑parity under realistic operating conditions.

Motivated by this, I set out to build a modelling tool that integrates device‑specific behaviour into yield predictions, enabling more accurate technical and economic assessments for next‑generation PVs.

Model Development

The modelling framework incorporates:

  • Adapted De Soto five‑parameter model for perovskite & organic PVs
  • Temperature‑dependent PCE behaviour using device‑specific coefficients
  • Irradiance‑dependent efficiency curves derived from literature datasets
  • Spectral mismatch modelling using FARMS‑NIT clear‑sky & cloudy‑sky spectra
  • Angle‑of‑incidence and reflection losses to capture optical effects

These additions allow accurate prediction of I–V characteristics and energy yield under realistic environmental conditions, improving both engineering and techno‑economic analysis.

Applications

The modelling tool supports:

  • Energy‑yield prediction under real‑world conditions
  • Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) analysis
  • UK grid suitability and system‑level assessments
  • Techno‑economic comparison between silicon and emerging PVs

The project aligns with my broader interest in how engineering assumptions influence commercial outcomes — from investment decisions to market competitiveness — and demonstrates my ability to build analytical tools that support evidence‑based decision‑making in the energy sector.
